## Why

Two tabs answered overlapping questions from opposite ends. **Image Finder** owns
the results *wall* (frames side by side on one absolute scale, reference-day
differencing, per-frame marks and adjustment, "Save comparison"), but its PV
Search could only mark time *regions* and was slow. **One Moment** owns the *PV
graph* and a frame resolver that is effectively instant.

The operator wants one tab: clicking a *point* in the PV graph gives that exact
moment across every picked camera, as fast as One Moment does it, while dragging
a *region* keeps working. One Moment is deleted only at the end, once everything
it does has a home in the Finder.

## Decisions taken (by the operator)

1. The PV graph **stays a separate window** opened from the `🎯 PV Search`
   button, modal, with OK / Cancel. A left click *marks* the moment inside the
   window; OK closes it and fills the wall.
2. The `Time window` button **stays** — one place where days and hours are picked.
3. Wall layout: all tiles **the same size as each other**, at most **4 per row**,
   and the size still **maximised** against the pane (2 frames come out bigger
   than 3).
4. Everything from One Moment is transferred **in stages**; `om_t.py` is deleted
   last.

## Stage 1 — one moment, every camera (DONE)

**Clicking a point.** `PVRegionSearchDialog` (`if_t.py`) gained:

* `_on_press` / `_on_release` — a left press-release inside `_PV_CLICK_SLOP_PX`
  (5 screen pixels) is a CLICK, not a drag. Measured in pixels, not seconds: a
  few seconds is an enormous drag on a zoomed axis and no movement across a week.
* `_set_moment_from_x` → `_snap_ns` — the moment snaps to the nearest real sample
  of the primary PV. A time between two samples has no shot behind it, so the
  frames pulled for it would be an arbitrary pick.
* `_moment_ns` is returned by `get_config()` as `moment_ns`; `_on_accept` accepts
  on a marked moment as well as on marked regions; `_sync_search_button` gained a
  third label, "🎯 Search this moment".
* A moment and a region are **mutually exclusive** — picking one clears the other,
  so the Search button never means two things.
* The picked moment (`_lbl_moment` + a `Clear` button) is **pinned below the
  scrolling sidebar**, beside the status line, because it is the answer the window
  exists to produce and must never be below the fold.
* `_paint_moment_cursor` draws a dashed line at the moment.
* Two `SpanSelector`s instead of one, bound one per mouse button (`_make_span`):
  **left drag marks a region, right drag zooms in, right click zooms back out one
  step** (`_zoom_out`, `_xlim_stack`). One selector left on its default answered
  BOTH buttons, so a right-drag meant as a zoom also marked a region.

**Finding the frames fast.** New in `if_t.py`:

* `_get_shot_finder_module()` — borrows `sf_t`. It publishes the already-loaded
  Slider under `"image_slider"` first, or running the Finder standalone would exec
  27k lines of `is_t.py` a second time (a second connection pool and day cache).
* `_resolve_moment_one(ts_ns, cam, scan_cache)` — a thin adapter over
  `sf_t._find_image_in_day`, the program's ONE frame-for-a-timestamp resolver.
* `_MomentSignals` / `_MomentResolveTask` — 16-wide fan-out over the cameras. One
  signals `QObject` per widget, never one per worker (the parented-signal leak).
* `ImageFinderWidget._scan_cache` = `sf_t.DayScanCache()`, threaded through every
  resolve. **This is where the speed comes from**: one `os.scandir` of an hour
  folder answers every camera, and one reading of a camera folder answers every
  later moment in that hour.
* `_res_cache_get` / `_res_cache_put` — `(cam, asked_ns)` → the resolved item, so
  a revisited moment costs nothing. **Negative-cache guard**: a `None` result for
  a moment younger than `_MOMENT_MISS_MIN_AGE_S` (600 s) is not cached, because
  the archiver runs behind and the frame may simply not be written yet.
* `_load_moment(ts_ns)`, `_on_moment_item`, `_on_moment_done` — resolve, then
  `fill_wall(..., moment_ns=ts_ns)`. `cancel_scan()` stops it (Stop All).

Measured in `test_finder_moment.py`: three cameras cold = **4 folder readings**;
asking again = **0**; a different moment in the same hour = **0**.

**Showing it honestly.**

* `fill_wall(results, cameras, moment_ns=None)` — with a moment it builds ONE
  wall (a tab per camera would each hold a single tile) titled `dd.mm. HH:MM:SS`,
  and it **keeps** cameras that had no frame (status `"no_frame"`). Without a
  moment, a camera with no frame is still dropped, or a many-day search would fill
  the wall with empty tiles for every day a camera did not run.
* `_DayWall._multi_cam` — many cameras at one moment → the caption names the
  CAMERA; many days of one camera → it names the DAY.
* A `no_frame` tile: dark red caption band with **white** ink (never a dark tint
  left to inherit black text) reading `<camera>  no frame`, and the reason in
  warning red in the picture area. `_paint_into` now tells three states apart —
  "nothing near this moment", "still being read", and "no picture".
* `_tile_tip(idx)` — hover tooltip with the camera, the day, the frame's own time
  and **the signed offset from the moment picked** (`+1.4 s from the moment
  picked`), plus the file path.
* `_on_moment_done` logs the cost: `found in X s, N folder read(s)` /
  `from memory`, and `N camera(s) had nothing near this moment`.

**Reading the frames.** `_DayWall._read_raw` / `_kick_load` now pull the bytes
into memory first (`is_t._read_frame_bytes`) and hand PIL a `BytesIO`, 8 frames in
parallel. Reading straight off the UNC path is the measured slow path, and going
through `_read_frame_bytes` brings the mid-write wait with it (it checks for the
PNG end marker), so a frame caught while the archiver is still writing it is
waited out instead of coming back broken.

**Bug fixed on the way.** `_open_pv_region_search` called `self._cal.selectedDate()`
— a panel calendar that moved into `daypicker` long ago. Opening PV Search with no
day picked raised `AttributeError`. Now `daypicker.date_to_qdate(self._primary_day())`.

## Stage 2 — the wall layout (DONE)

`_DayWall` gained a third layout mode, **`"grid"`**, now the default for every
per-camera wall and for the moment wall. `"rows"` (the Day-by-day tab) is
unchanged. `"pack"` — the Slider's free-form partition, where the biggest frame
wins — is kept but no longer selected by default.

`_relayout_grid()`:

1. **Fit** — for `cols` in `1 … min(4, n)`, score = the area of the **smallest
   drawn picture**; keep the best, ties to fewer rows. Every cell identical, each
   picture centred in its cell at its own aspect ratio.
2. **Zoom** — the cell grows by exactly the zoom factor and as many as fit go in a
   row; leftover width is split **evenly on both sides**, so the row reads as
   centred.

`_MAX_COLS = 4`, `_ZOOM_MIN/_MAX = 0.5/8.0`, `_ZOOM_STEP = 1.15`.
`set_zoom` / `zoom_by_notches` / `reset_zoom`; `_WallScroll(QScrollArea)` turns
**Ctrl+wheel** into a zoom (accepting the event so the pane does not also scroll)
and a **Fit** button in Image / Display resets it, showing the current percentage.

**`set_canvas(w, h)` — read this before touching the layout.** The wall cannot
work out its own pane size: zoomed in it is deliberately BIGGER than the pane, so
a fit measured against its own size sees the size the last pass asked for and
grows again, every pass. So the pane **states** its size and `_WallScroll.resizeEvent`
calls `set_canvas` from its viewport. Two other approaches were tried and both
fail: Qt does not shrink a widget back when its minimum is relaxed (the stretched
height lingers and reads as a huge pane), and a scroll area that has not been laid
out yet answers with a 638×478 placeholder viewport.

Two judgement calls, both because the obvious alternatives were worse:

* **The zoomed row is centred.** Snapping the cell up to fill the row exactly gave
  five notches in a row that changed nothing (every width between "one tile fills
  the pane" and "one tile is wider than it" snaps to the same thing). Leaving the
  leftover at the right-hand end put up to half the pane black beside a single
  tile.
* **3 and 4 frames come out the same size.** Both land on two-by-two, and on a
  typical pane that really is the largest the smallest frame can be — three in a
  row would make every one of them smaller. Measured, 1200×800 pane, 4:3 frames:
  1 frame 798 768 px², 2 → 264 627, 3 → 186 501, 4 → 186 501, 5 → 116 427,
  9 → 77 441, 13 → 40 368.

## Stage 2b — one mark on every frame, and icons (DONE)

Marks were always stored as **fractions of the frame they are drawn on** (a circle
is a centre and two radii between 0 and 1), so "the same point on every frame" is
copying those numbers across.

* `_WallShared.link_marks`, **on by default**; checkbox **"Same spot on every
  frame"** in Image / Display (`_on_link_marks_toggled`).
* `_DayWall._mirror_marks(src_path)` — called from `_begin_overlay` and
  `_drag_overlay`, so the mark grows on all tiles **during** the drag, not on
  release. Handles work from any tile, since every frame carries the same numbers.
* `clear_overlays(paths)` clears everywhere while linked — otherwise the next drag
  would mirror the mark straight back and the menu entry would look broken.
* `_begin_overlay` now takes one `push_undo()` per gesture, so Undo takes a mark
  off every frame in one step.
* Turning the switch ON does **not** reach back and copy existing marks: it changes
  what the next mark does, so a frame deliberately marked on its own is not
  overwritten.

**What a fraction means, said in the tooltip rather than implied**: over many days
of ONE camera it is the same sensor pixel; across cameras of different shape it is
the same *relative* point, not the same distance; and the fractions are of the
frame AS DRAWN, so a frame turned 90° wears its mark at the same place on screen,
not on the sensor.

**Icons.** `_set_action_icon(btn, name)` + `_get_workshop_module()` in `if_t.py`
borrow `wk_t.action_icon` — the program's one painted-icon vocabulary. Four new
recipes in `wk_t._ICON_RECIPES_ACTION`: `mark_circle`, `mark_square`,
`mark_cross`, `marks_clear`, each the shape inside a picture frame (these buttons
put a reference mark ON a frame; the frame is half of what they mean).
`undo`, `reset`, `rotate_left`, `rotate_right` already existed.

The two turning icons are mirror images and **cannot be told apart at 16 px**, so
the direction stays in the words: **`Left 90°` / `Right 90°`**. Verified by
rendering every icon at 16 / 20 / 32 / 64 px, enabled and greyed, on the panel and
on a button ground.

## Stage 3 — the region and condition searches at the same speed (DONE)

The day-and-region search asked the archiver and the share for the same things over
and over, one after another. Three changes, all in `if_t.py`:

* **`_resolve_frame_at` is now the one frame-for-a-timestamp resolver** for every
  search — the same `_resolve_moment_one` adapter Stage 1 built, over the shared
  `DayScanCache` (`_ensure_scan_cache`). It replaced `_frame_nearest_ns`, which
  guessed file names and asked the share whether each existed: up to 132 round trips
  across a ±2 s window, per camera, per region, sharing nothing with anything else.
  `_frame_nearest_ns` is gone; `_nearest_file_for_ns` stays, the automatic
  TotalPower search still uses it.
* **`_region_targets` reads the primary PV once per day.** It works out WHEN each
  marked region points at — the peak sample, or the region's midpoint when the PV has
  nothing there — and knows nothing about cameras, which is the point: the samples in
  a region are the same whichever camera is being looked for.
  `_find_image_for_regions` takes those targets as an argument (and still reads the
  PV itself when called without them, so it works alone).
* **`_run_search_units` runs the (day × camera) units in parallel** —
  `_SEARCH_UNIT_WORKERS` = 8, and `_SEARCH_DAY_WORKERS` = 6 for the per-day PV reads.
  It is a METHOD, not the closure it was inside `_run_multiday_search`, so it can be
  run and counted without a modal progress window. The condition search fans its
  cameras out the same way, 16 at a time.

## The camera picker's Auto-arrange (found today, NOT part of the plan — DONE)

Reported: for one camera set, pressing a **size button** and pressing
**Auto-arrange** give two different arrangements, and both leave a lot of black
space. **Nothing in stages 1–2b touches this** — it is `is_t.py`
`compute_camera_layout` and the picker board, neither of which was modified.

Diagnosed and measured (set: `PASF1NF, PASF2NF, WRT2DPNF, PFM13NF, PAM10NF,
PTM11wNF`; sizes large/medium/small from `cam_sizes.json`; canvas 1424×782):

* The packer tries only **five camera orders**, all derived from the order it is
  handed — and the two buttons hand it different orders. `_on_auto_arrange` resets
  `_pack_order` to the selected-list order; `_on_size_changed` calls
  `_reset_tiles()` and leaves `_pack_order` as `_seed_board_from_saved` set it,
  which for this set is the `cam_order` of the hand-made record in
  `cam_layouts.json`. Different starting order → different candidates → different
  picture, same set and same sizes.
* The size **ratios are fine** in both — large : medium : small = 12 : 8 : 4 to
  under 1 %.
* The **area is not**. Over all 720 orders: 328 reach **76.4–76.5 %** of the
  canvas, 392 land on **67.2 %** — and both orders the app uses are in the 67.2 %
  bucket. That is **14 % more image area** left on the table (about 7 % in each
  direction).
* Cost is not the obstacle: one cold arrangement of 6 cameras is **4–15 ms**,
  0.02 ms warm (cached per canvas size). The operator has said 100–200 ms is fine.

### What was done

The numbers below are from this machine's own `cam_aspects.json`, so they differ a
little from the ones first reported (65.0 % against 74.0 %, not 67.2 / 76.5); it is
the same set and the same two buckets.

**1. The search runs over a POOL of camera orders** — `_layout_order_pool` in
`is_t.py`. From each sensible sorting (as handed in, by shape both ways, by size
both ways) it also takes that sorting's **reverse**, its **rotations**, and the
order **alternating between its two ends** (biggest, smallest, next biggest …).
Each of those changes who is NEXT TO WHOM, which is the only thing the packing
cares about. `_layout_order_cap` stops at 16 orders, 12 above ten cameras.

Random shuffles were tried first and are **worse**: they need about twice as many
orders to reach the same answer, never got there at all on eleven and twelve
cameras (66–67 % against 71 % for the same number of orders), and they make the
result depend on a seed. Measured, not assumed.

**2. Nothing in the pool is built from the order handed in.** Every order in it
comes from the cameras' own shapes and sizes. That is the half of the bug that made
the two buttons disagree — and it means the two searches cannot diverge again, not
just that they happen to agree today. Which camera lands in which of two equally
good tiles still follows the order handed in, as the LAST tie-break
(`_reading_order_cost`), so the cameras read in the order they were picked.

**3. `_on_size_changed` packs in the picked list's order**, exactly as
`_on_auto_arrange` does. Both halves were needed: point 2 makes the arrangement the
same, point 3 makes the cameras sit in the same tiles.

**4. The arrangements are cached per CAMERA SET, not per canvas size**
(`_layout_tree_pool` / `_LAYOUT_TREE_CACHE`). Which arrangements exist depends on
the shapes and the sizes, not on how big the window is, so a resize now pays only
for scoring them. This is what pays for the wider search: building the trees is the
slow half (0.4 ms per order at six cameras, 45 ms at twelve) and it used to run
again for every size the window passed through. A wide pool is also screened with
two correction rounds instead of four and twice as long a shortlist
(`_LAYOUT_SIZE_WIDE_POOL`) — same arrangement, half the time — and the canvas size
is rounded to about a percent of itself before it is looked up.

Measured, same canvas, before → after (picture area as a share of the canvas, the
size ratios unchanged and `_size_order_kept` true throughout):

| cameras | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 65.0 % | 77.8 % | 73.0 % | 70.7 % | 73.4 % | 62.7 % | 64.9 % |
| after | **74.0 %** | **80.0 %** | 73.0 % | **74.7 %** | 73.4 % | **66.8 %** | **66.3 %** |

Cost: the first arrangement of a camera set is slower (6 cameras 24 → 46 ms, 12
cameras 160 → 706 ms, once, when the picker opens or a size is pressed); every
window size after that is faster where it used to hurt — 12 cameras 224 → 52 ms
per resize step, 6 cameras 21 → 47 ms.

**Why not a complete search.** A subset DP over all 2ⁿ camera groupings was written
and measured: it reaches the same 73.9 % on six cameras but takes 0.4 s at eight
and 11 s at ten, and it is NOT better — its per-group pruning throws away trees the
order-based search keeps, so on seven cameras it came out at 74.9 % where the order
pool reaches 80.0 %. Abandoned.

**Judge an arrangement by two numbers, never one.** For a set with sizes set, the
code's own criterion is *first* how close the frames come to the ratio asked for
(`_size_mismatch`, filtered to within 1.15×), and only then how much picture is on
screen. A wider pool can therefore legitimately show LESS picture — by finding a
closer size fit. Comparing pools on fill alone is how you conclude that a search is
worse when it is doing exactly what it was told.

Pinned by `testing/test_auto_arrange.py` (17 checks): the canvas is filled, the
order handed in changes neither the tiles nor any camera's picture, both buttons
agree (driven through the real picker with a remembered hand-made arrangement
seeded, which is what made them disagree), two cold runs are identical, the sizes
are still honoured, and eight further window sizes rebuild nothing.

Still passing: `test_cam_size_layout.py`, `test_cam_picker_wysiwyg.py`,
`test_size_grid_render.py`, and the stage 1–2b tests.

## Stage 4 — the graph (STARTED: axes and hold-forward done)

Done and pinned by `test_pv_graph.py` (18 checks):

* **The per-PV normalisation is gone.** `_redraw` no longer divides by each PV's own
  maximum. What is drawn is what was archived.
* **One y axis per unit.** `_unit_key_for` reads the unit from the shared PV registry
  through a channel → registry-name map (`_registry_name_for`); the first unit keeps
  the main axes (it owns x, so the spans, the moment cursor and the zoom stay with
  it), each further unit gets a `twinx` with its spine set outward. A PV with no unit
  sits on its own axis, since nothing says its numbers are the same kind of thing as
  anything else's. One legend is built from every axis's handles — matplotlib would
  otherwise stack a legend per axes in the same corner.
* **Hold-forward** (`_hold_xy` + `_seeds`): a channel the archiver writes only when it
  CHANGES is carried across the day from the value it was already sitting at, and on
  to the right edge (or to now, on today). The verdict comes from
  `cpva.classify_step_channel` with `primary=` the day just fetched, so it usually
  costs no extra request; the value before the day comes from `cpva.value_at_or_before`
  and is fetched only for channels that hold. Curves are drawn `steps-post`, and the
  synthetic edge points carry no marker.
* **Colour and line shape belong to the PV** (`_style_for`), taken from its place in
  the PV list, not from the draw order — and past the tenth PV the line changes shape
  instead of a colour repeating (with six colours and seven PVs, two curves came out
  identical; seen in the render, then fixed).

**The trap this cost, worth not paying twice:** the step verdict and the
value-before-the-day are a SECOND pass in the loader, after the graph is already
drawn. Deciding them inline made the day's load wait on the archiver even though the
samples were already in hand — with the archiver slow or unreachable the window came
up with no curves at all, and a click then picked a raw time instead of snapping to a
sample. The first draw uses `cpva.is_step_channel(ch)` without a timestamp, which
answers from what is already known and never asks; the refined verdict and the seeds
arrive in a second emit and redraw. It showed up as `test_finder_moment.py` failing
intermittently, not as anything visible on screen.

Still to do in Stage 4: the range statistics table with the held `n = 0` row, the
formula-over-time engine, prev/next shot stepping (arrows in the TAB's panel), copy /
save the graph, and the settings controls above the graph that replace the menus.

## Stage 4 — what the rest of the port has to reckon with (surveyed)

The graph engine was read out of `om_t.py` before touching it. Four things decide how
the port has to be done, and none of them is obvious from the plan:

1. **The two graphs disagree about what x IS.** The Finder's PV window draws days:
   `_ns_to_x` / `_x_to_ns` / `_day_for_x` (`if_t.py` ~8302-8335) hand matplotlib a
   `mdates` number in *one day* mode and `base + day index + fraction` in *all days*
   mode. One Moment draws seconds since Prague midnight against `_axis_t0_ns`, and
   `_hold_xy`, `_fmt_hms` and its `MultipleLocator` ticks all assume that. **The port
   must keep the Finder's x mapping and re-express the om_t drawing in it** — moving
   `_axis_t0_ns` over instead would break the day arrows, the region marking, the
   moment snapping and the compressed multi-day axis.
2. **The two graphs disagree about what a PV IS.** The Finder holds
   `{day: {channel: [(t_ns, value), …]}}` — plain lists, channels off a checkbox list,
   no units, no scale, no formulas. One Moment holds `{pv name: {ts, val, channel,
   status, step, seed, good}}` as numpy arrays, keyed by the is_t PV registry. Per-unit
   axes, hold-forward and the formula engine all need the second shape, so the port
   brings the is_t registry (`pv_units_for`, `PV_SCALE`, `PV_DERIVED`, …) with it. This
   is the bulk of the work, not the drawing.
3. **The normalisation to drop is in `if_t.py`, not `om_t.py`** — `_redraw` divides by
   the per-channel maximum (~`if_t.py:8474`) and labels the axis "PV value (normalized
   per PV)" (~8502). One Moment never normalised anything.
4. **A dozen helpers already exist on both sides** and must be merged rather than
   copied: the matplotlib toolbar and its icon repaint, the span selectors, the
   click-versus-drag slop, `_toolbar_busy`, the zoom stack, the moment snap, the moment
   cursor, and two different status vocabularies (`_PV_STATUS_RANK` has `empty`,

* A tile stuck on an old frame must retry by itself.
* A display control hits the frames selected **when the control was moved**.
* A PV's curve colour comes from the PV, never from its index in the list.

  `WA_StyledBackground` before a stylesheet background is drawn at all.
